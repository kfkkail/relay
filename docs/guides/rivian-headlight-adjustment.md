# How to check and adjust Rivian headlight aim

If your Rivian’s low beams seem too high or too low, start by checking their aim on a level surface facing a wall. Before turning an adjuster, get the aiming procedure for your exact model, model year, headlamp hardware, and market from Rivian. A generic “two inches down at 25 feet” rule is not a verified Rivian specification.

This guide explains the checking process and how to apply a confirmed adjustment procedure. The vehicle’s model and year were not provided, so adjuster location, tool size, turning direction, target slope, and any calibration requirements are intentionally not specified. Do not assume that instructions for one R1T or R1S apply to another generation or to another Rivian model.

## 1. Confirm what needs changing

- **Beam aim:** The low-beam cutoff consistently appears too high, too low, or uneven when checked under the correct conditions.
- **Lighting settings:** Automatic high-beam behavior is a separate issue. Review the lighting controls in your vehicle’s owner’s guide before making a mechanical adjustment.
- **A fault or damage:** A lighting warning, loose lamp, damaged mounting, sudden change after an impact, or unstable beam needs inspection. Adjusting aim may conceal the underlying problem.

Flashes from oncoming drivers alone do not establish that your lights need adjustment; road crests, loading, and high-beam operation can also affect glare.

## 2. Get the vehicle-specific instructions

Open [Rivian’s manuals and guides](https://rivian.com/support/support-documents) and choose the document for your vehicle. Look for lighting, headlamps, and headlight aiming. If the owner’s guide does not give a mechanical aiming procedure, ask Rivian Service for guidance or book an aim check.

Before proceeding, confirm:

- The test distance and the point on the vehicle from which to measure it.
- Required tire pressure, loading, suspension height, and leveling conditions.
- Which part of the low-beam cutoff is the aiming reference and its required position.
- Whether owner adjustment is supported, which adjuster to use, and the correct tool.
- Whether a diagnostic mode, leveling initialization, or calibration is required.

If these details are unavailable, complete the observational check below and have Rivian perform the adjustment.

## 3. Prepare a repeatable check

You will need a tape measure, painter’s tape, a flat vertical wall, a genuinely level parking surface, and enough darkness to see the beam cutoff clearly. You only need an adjustment tool once the correct procedure identifies it.

1. Clean the headlamp lenses and set cold tire pressures to the vehicle placard or the specified test conditions.
2. Remove unusual cargo and follow the prescribed passenger/load conditions. Do not aim the lights to compensate for a temporary heavy load or trailer.
3. Set the suspension to the specified aiming height and let it settle. Avoid entry/exit or other automatic height changes during the check; follow Rivian’s instructions for maintaining the test height.
4. Park square to the wall, with the wheels straight. Select Park and keep the vehicle secure.
5. Select low beams using the vehicle’s lighting controls. Ensure high beams and automatic high-beam activation will not interfere with the check.

A sloped driveway can make correctly aimed lights look wrong. If the surface is not level, use a suitable location or a professional beam setter.

## 4. Mark and measure

1. Identify each headlamp’s optical reference point using the vehicle-specific procedure. Do not use the decorative light bar as the aiming reference.
2. Measure each reference point’s height above the ground. Transfer those heights to the wall with horizontal tape marks, and mark the lateral position of each lamp while keeping the vehicle square to the wall.
3. Position the vehicle at the specified test distance, measured from the specified datum. Recheck alignment and suspension height.
4. Mark the target cutoff position using the prescribed offset or slope.
5. Photograph the existing cutoff and record its position before making changes. Evaluate each beam independently using the method specified by Rivian; do not rest a cover on an illuminated lens.

If the procedure specifies a downward percentage slope, the geometry is:

**Vertical drop = test distance × slope / 100**, using the same units for distance and drop.

For example, a *hypothetical* 1% downward slope at 25 feet gives a 3-inch drop. This demonstrates the calculation; it is **not a Rivian aiming specification**. Use the specified cutoff segment, not the brightest part of the beam or an upward step in its pattern. Do not assume both beam patterns should be identical.

## 5. Adjust only with the confirmed procedure

1. Follow the specified access instructions and identify the vertical aiming adjuster positively. Do not loosen mounting bolts or turn an unidentified screw.
2. Record the initial position and follow the prescribed adjustment increments. Observe the cutoff after each movement; do not assume a clockwise direction universally raises or lowers it.
3. Bring the specified cutoff reference to the target. Follow any separate left/right requirements. Do not alter horizontal aim unless the procedure explicitly calls for it.
4. Stop if the adjuster binds, the beam does not respond, the lamp moves loosely, or the target cannot be reached within normal travel.
5. Complete any required initialization or calibration and replace any removed access covers according to the procedure.

If special equipment or a service mode is required, have Rivian Service finish the work. A wall check cannot substitute for a required calibration.

## 6. Verify the result

Repeat the wall check after the suspension has settled, under the same distance, loading, and height conditions. Confirm both lamps operate and there are no lighting warnings. Record the final measurements and adjustment made.

A brief drive on a familiar road can reveal an obvious problem, but perceived brightness or the absence of oncoming flashes does not prove correct aim. If visibility remains poor, glare persists, or the cutoff changes unexpectedly, arrange a professional aim and leveling-system check.

## What to send Rivian Service

> Please check the low-beam aim on my [model, year, VIN]. The issue is [too high / too low / uneven / changing]. It occurs at [ride height and load]. I have attached photos of both beams on a level wall and recorded the test distance. Please confirm the correct aiming specification and whether leveling or calibration is needed.
